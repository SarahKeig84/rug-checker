    How recommendations are made

    Rug Checker turns the forecast into a “felt” temperature for your horse, then suggests the fill weight that would keep them comfortable, and finally picks the closest match from your rugs.

    1. Forecast for each period (turnout or stabled), weighted toward the coldest hours – you rug for the overnight low, not the afternoon high.
    2. Adjustments for clip, breed type, age, body condition, and (when turned out) wind, rain and how sheltered the field is.
    3. Comfort floor of ~5°C. Healthy adult horses are widely quoted as comfortable between about 5°C and 25°C (their thermoneutral zone), and well-adapted, unclipped horses cope with much lower. Below a felt 5°C, roughly 25g of fill is suggested per degree.
    4. Lean lighter. Over-rugging is a common welfare problem, so the app favours slightly lighter options – unless your horse is elderly or in poor condition, when it leans warmer.

    Key guidance behind the app

    • Not every horse needs a rug. A healthy horse with a full winter coat, forage and shelter copes with cold well. Their coat fluffs up to trap warm air; a rug flattens it.
    • Clipping removes protection, so clipped horses need rugs to replace the coat taken away – more coat removed means more rug needed.
    • Wind and rain are what really chill a horse, especially in an exposed field. Shelter (hedges, trees, field shelter) makes a big difference.
    • Natural winter weight loss is healthy for good-doers. Over-rugging and over-feeding override it and contribute to obesity and laminitis risk.
    • Signs of over-rugging: sweating or damp coat under the rug, restlessness, rubbing, skin problems. Horses start sweating around 25°C, the top of their comfort zone.
    • Veterans, poor-doers, thin-skinned breeds (Thoroughbreds, Arabs) and horses that are unwell feel the cold more. Donkeys’ coats are not waterproof.
    • Health conditions change things. Horses recovering from illness, eating less forage, or with arthritis may need more warmth; PPID, anhidrosis and laminitis-prone horses are more at risk from over-rugging. Your vet’s advice for your horse always comes first.
    • Check, don’t guess: feel behind the withers under the rug; remove rugs daily to check skin, condition and fit.
